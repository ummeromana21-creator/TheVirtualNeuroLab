Where the lab goes from here

The lab has made its first predictions about a real fly brain. Some can only be settled in a real fly. Some of the next steps are already under way, and some are plans. Each item below says which it is.

Predictions waiting for a real-fly test

The Knockout Lab predicts that the fly needs these neurons to eat or drink. A simulation can't prove that; only an experiment on living flies can. Each card says how sure we are and why. Full details of every run are in the lab notebook.

How a fly lab could test one.
  1. Switch the neuron off in living flies, using a genetic tool that targets just that cell type.
  2. Offer the flies water (or sugar) and count how often they extend their proboscis to drink.
  3. Compare with flies whose neuron still works. If drinking drops, the prediction holds; if not, the model has missed something, and that's worth knowing too.
Whether a tool exists today that reaches only one of these cell types is something a fly lab would need to check.

Experiments on the worm

The worm is in the lab with all 302 neurons and every known connection. Before any experiment, its simulated nervous system needs its own calibration test, for example the gentle-touch reflex mapped in living worms in 1985.

Next experiments on the fly

Planned

More behaviours

The same switch-off approach works for any behaviour with a known motor or command neuron: grooming the antennae (round 1 already checks its trigger), escaping, walking, and the male's courtship song.

Planned

Switching neurons on, not just off

Screen which single neurons can start a behaviour by themselves, then compare with real flies where results exist.

Planned

Chemicals inside the simulation

Today the model has wiring but no chemical messengers such as dopamine or the signals for hunger and thirst. Adding them would let the lab test what a substance does, not only what wiring does.

More organisms

Wiring maps now exist for parts of several animals. Each would join the lab the way the worm did: anatomy first, then a calibration test, then experiments.
