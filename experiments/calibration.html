Experiments · Calibration test

Does sugar make the fly stick out its tongue?

Before the lab runs any new experiment, its brain has to get a known one right. When a fly tastes sugar, it sticks out its proboscis (its tongue) to drink. Bitter taste stops that. Both are proven in living flies. Here we test whether the simulated FlyWire brain does the same, with the pass/fail rules written down before the run.

What we did, in plain words

  1. Switch on the sugar-taste neurons on one side of the fly's mouthparts, the way scientists do in real flies with light-activated switches. We try gentle to strong (10 to 200 signals per second).
  2. Watch MN9, the motor neuron that pushes the proboscis out. If MN9 fires, the fly would stick out its tongue.
  3. Repeat with bitter taste on its own, bitter together with sugar, and water-taste neurons.
  4. Every condition is run 30 times for one simulated second each, and the firing is averaged.

The rules, written before the run

Saved 3 Nov 2026 at 14:26 UTC, before the run. Nothing in the model was changed after seeing results.

  • C1Sugar neurons at 100 per second make MN9 fire 10 or more times per second.
  • C2More sugar, more MN9: firing rises from 10 to 200 sugar signals per second.
  • C3Bitter alone (200 per second) does not make MN9 fire (under 1 per second).
  • C4Bitter (200) added to sugar (100) cuts MN9 firing by at least half.
  • C5Water neurons at 200 per second make MN9 fire 5 or more times per second.
  • C6Activity stays contained: fewer than 2,000 neurons fire for sugar or for bitter.

Every result

Can the engine be trusted?

Read with care. These are model firing rates, not measurements from a living fly; only the direction and shape of each effect is compared with real flies. "Sticks out its tongue" means "the motor neuron that would do it fires".

Real-fly evidence this is checked against

  • Activating sugar taste neurons (Gr5a) makes flies extend the proboscis; activating bitter neurons (Gr66a) does not, and suppresses extension. (Marella et al. 2006, Neuron 49:285)
  • MN9 is a motor neuron that drives proboscis extension and responds to sugar. (Gordon & Scott 2009, Neuron 61:373)
  • Water-sensing neurons (ppk28) drive drinking. (Cameron et al. 2010, Nature 465:91)
  • The same model on the FlyWire brain: sugar neurons at 100 Hz drive MN9 strongly; bitter suppresses it; 91% of 164 predictions held in living flies. (Shiu et al. 2024, Nature 634:210)
