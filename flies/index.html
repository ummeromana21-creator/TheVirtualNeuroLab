Behaviours not yet modelled

The fly shows only behaviours whose control neurons the brain model reads out, each checked against real flies. These need more than the brain we have today, so they are not shown rather than faked.

WalkingLeg movement is coordinated in the nerve cord, which is not in the FlyWire brain. The brain's walking commands could be read next, after their own calibration test.
EscapeTriggered by something looming in view. It needs input to the visual system, which has not been tested in this model.
FlightWing control lives almost entirely in the nerve cord.
Courtship songOnly males sing; FlyWire is a female brain.

Body: NeuroMechFly (Lobato-Rios et al. 2022, Nature Methods; flygym, Apache-2.0), reconstructed from a micro-CT scan of an adult fruit fly; shape and joints from the scan, colours added here, shown in NeuroMechFly's neutral standing pose. Brain: FlyWire v783 (Dorkenwald et al. 2024; Schlegel et al. 2024) running the published model of Shiu et al. 2024, unchanged. Taste stimuli are the Taste Bench runs (10 repeats each); antenna touch is Round 1 experiment E7 (Johnston's organ CE neurons, 30 repeats). Behaviour rules were written before the runs: tongue out when MN9 fires at least 10 times a second; antennal grooming command when aBN1 fires at least 5 times a second (aBN1 starts antennal grooming in real flies, Hampel et al. 2015). Each neuron is placed in the region where it has most of its synapses; a region's brightness is the share of its neurons that fired. The movements are animations of the behaviour the brain's output calls for; the body itself is not simulated. Data: behaviour.json.
