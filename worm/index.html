Second organism · Caenorhabditis elegans

The worm: a whole nervous system you can hold in one view

C. elegans is a tiny roundworm, about 1 mm long. It was the first animal whose complete wiring diagram was mapped, and every adult hermaphrodite has the same 302 neurons with the same names. Here they are in 3D, wired with every known connection. Click any neuron to see who it talks to.

What you're looking at

The neurons

  • Sensory neurons feel touch, taste, smell and temperature. Many sit in the head.
  • Interneurons sit in between and make decisions. Most cluster in the nerve ring, the worm's "brain", a loop of wiring around its throat.
  • Motor neurons run along the belly and back and make the body wave, so the worm crawls forward or backs away.

The connections

  • Chemical connections pass a signal one way, from sender to receiver.
  • Electrical connections (gap junctions) are direct channels between two cells, so signals can pass both ways.
  • The number on each partner is how many connections were counted between the two cells in the electron-microscope reconstruction.
Experiments on the worm: in progress. For now this page shows the worm's real anatomy and wiring. Running experiments on it, like the Neuron Silencing Screen does for the fly, needs its own calibration test against real-worm results first.

Data. Neuron shapes and positions: the Virtual Worm 3D model by Christian Grove and Paul Sternberg (WormBase, Caltech), released into the public domain and converted to NeuroML by the OpenWorm project (MIT licence). The shapes are a hand-built model drawn to match the worm's known anatomy, not traced from one electron-microscopy dataset, so fine detail is approximate. Wiring: the adult hermaphrodite connectome of Cook et al. 2019 (Nature 571:63), as packaged by OpenWorm c302 (herm_full_edgelist.csv); only neuron-to-neuron connections are shown. Gentle-touch circuit: Chalfie et al. 1985 (J Neurosci 5:956). The model worm lies in a crawling pose, head on the left.
